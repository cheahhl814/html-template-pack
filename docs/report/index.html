    Methodology

    PERIOD · JUL–SEP 2026 · INTERNAL CIRCULATION

    The Q3 figures in this report are sourced from three systems, joined in the central data warehouse overnight on 2026-10-02.

    1
    Transport management system (TMS)
    Source of truth for on-time delivery, transit times, and carrier performance.
    2
    Warehouse management system (WMS)
    Hourly pick/put-away events; utilisation is computed as peak-hour throughput / rated capacity.
    3
    HRIS labour data
    Hours-worked and headcount, joined to warehouse events via employee badge IDs.
    4
    Finance margin adjustments
    Operating margin excludes one-off restructuring costs (€1.2M in August) for trend-comparable figures.

    Why delivery improved

    The Q-over-Q delivery improvement was driven by a single change: the dispatch algorithm deployed to all hubs on 2026-08-04. The causal chain is shown below.

    Driver — Q2 carrier scorecard disputes caused manual dispatch overrides at 3 hubs
    ↓
    Mediator — new dispatch algorithm auto-balances loads against carrier scorecards
    ↓
    Outcome — on-time delivery rises 4.7 pp; carrier scorecard disputes drop to zero in Q3
    FINDINGS

    Findings

    PERIOD · JUL–SEP 2026 · INTERNAL CIRCULATION

    Three findings need attention before the November planning cycle. Cards below summarise each; the most actionable item is the Rotterdam capacity decision.

    Headline result

    📈 On-time delivery rose 4.7 percentage points quarter-on-quarter

    The improvement is statistically significant against the 18-month rolling baseline (95% CI for the delta: [+3.1 pp, +6.3 pp]). It is also broadly distributed — every hub improved by at least 2.0 pp, suggesting the lift is systemic, not concentrated in one region.

    Secondary findings

    ✓ Carrier scorecard disputes dropped to zero

    Q2 had 14 disputed scoring events across 4 carriers. Q3 had none — the new quarterly review cadence and the auto-balancing dispatch algorithm together eliminated the dispute pathway.

    ⚠ Rotterdam at 94% utilisation

    Rotterdam peaked at 94% in week 11 — within 6 points of the operational ceiling. Any further volume growth in Q4 will require overtime (estimated €0.4M per quarter) or third-party overflow capacity (€0.6M per quarter).

    💡 Labour productivity pilot results

    The two-week pilot in Lyon (10–24 September) showed a 6.4% throughput lift per FTE when picks were routed by zone affinity rather than FCFS. Decision: extend the pilot through Q4 before a full rollout.

    💡 Madrid monitoring

    Madrid's on-time rate dipped below the 90% threshold for the first time since 2024-Q1. WMS data shows the slowdown is at inbound dock doors, not outbound. Recommend a site visit in November.

    How annotations work

    PROJECT / EVENT LABEL

    Each note stores the highlighted quote (or the caret position for inserts) plus its surrounding text and nearest heading, so it re-anchors even if the document is regenerated with minor changes. Diagrams and code (anything inside .mermaid, svg, or a data-annot-skip element) are ignored, so rendering them never shifts your highlights. Exported JSON carries the type (comment / delete / insert / replace) and a replacement field for structured edits — downstream revision agents don't need to parse free-text intent.

    The v2 engine reads each [data-panel] to know which section a highlight belongs to. When you click a drawer entry, the engine switches the matching tab before scrolling the highlight into view, so reviews can hop between findings and methodology without losing their place.
